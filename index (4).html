Where it started

Around 2013, Mo and Faisal found Sea Spray wasting away in Hurghada's New Marina — the home port of the MV Tala, the Red Sea Explorers dive liveaboard and a legend in her own right. Sea Spray was a Herreshoff H28, built in 1961 and sailed from California to the Red Sea in the 1990s. They bought her knowing almost nothing about sailing and learned it the hard way — books, YouTube, and long days on the water — until they could sail her themselves.

Friends came along, then more friends, and weekends grew into a community. In 2015, Faisal Khalaf and Hazem Moussa turned it into something real: Red Sea Sails.

The roots run deeper. Red Sea Explorers, the technical-diving liveaboard running since 2005, is still going today — and RSS and RSE have been inseparable ever since. Through every lean season, it has been RSE keeping an eye on RSS, helping her weather the storms.

Where we operate

Egypt's Red Sea coast
from El Gouna to Somabay.

A stretch of coastline with the conditions serious sailors want and the infrastructure serious owners need. 15–20 knots of thermal wind most days, flat water inside the reef, 300+ sailable days a year. Air 20–30°C, water 22–28°C. The season runs right through winter — with November to April as our peak, exactly when the Med is closed.

Sailing here isn't new — Egypt's yacht clubs date to the monarchy era, a century back. They've faded to dinghy-only over the decades. RSS is rebuilding cruiser and catamaran sailing on this coast: the school, the fleet, the regattas.

Reliable wind
Thermal breeze most days, predictable seasons, clean water and steady conditions.
4–5h from EU hubs
Direct flights into Hurghada from most European cities. No jet lag, no reposition.
Marina infrastructure
Abu Tig marina in El Gouna; Somabay's new marina completing this year.
Two bases
El Gouna and Somabay — both marina towns and event bases, an hour apart. Somabay is our upcoming full base.

Somabay · An hour south

Private peninsula.
New marina. New chapter.

A private peninsula an hour south of El Gouna. Steady cross-shore wind, protected bay, reef diving straight off the jetty, and its own kite and wingfoil stations. The new marina completes this year — where we ran the Somabay Sailing Festival in May and where we're expanding operations from October.

El Gouna to Somabay is an hour by road — or a 50nm day sail past islands, reefs and deserted anchorages. It's our flotilla and regatta route, with great diving and quiet islands the whole way down. Somabay isn't just an event base: from October it becomes a full year-round operation.

Somabay Sailing Festival · May 2026

Fifteen boats.
Eight catamarans.

Our first festival at Somabay. And for the first time at one of our events, catamarans outnumbered monohulls on the start line.

It's a small number in absolute terms — but it's a directional signal. Egyptian sailing families are moving to catamarans faster than the industry expected, and the reason is cultural: cats carry the extended family and friends the way monohulls never could. Add the growing regatta appetite and this becomes a real emerging market.
